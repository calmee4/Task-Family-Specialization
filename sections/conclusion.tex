\section{Conclusion}

We reanalyzed \gittaskbench using only its official \processmetric and
\resultmetric outputs. On the evaluated cohorts, the benchmark's success mass
is narrow, domain-skewed, and family-specific. Tied global totals hide material
specialization, and official \processmetric$=$True, \resultmetric$=$False rows
cluster in structured extraction families rather than behaving like random
noise. A targeted provider cohort shows that broader frontier coverage also
remains family-selective rather than collapsing into one uniform strong-model
baseline, the supplementary open-model breadth cohort reproduces the same
family concentration on a wider roster, and the full-benchmark provider sweeps
show that short subsets can misstate where provider gains actually land. The
paper therefore supports a
simple measurement recommendation:
repo-grounded agent evaluations should report the official
\processmetric/\resultmetric split and family-level slices, not only one global
pass rate.

More broadly, the paper argues for a modest shift in how benchmark results are
communicated. Future repo-grounded agent papers do not need new judges,
taxonomies, or benchmarks to become more informative. They do need to report
benchmark-visible intermediate structure instead of hiding it behind one
aggregate number. If a method improves only one task family, or mainly converts
\processmetric$=$False traces into gradeable-but-unsuccessful ones, that
pattern should remain visible in the report.

That recommendation matters even more for incremental repo-grounded agent
papers. Readers need to know whether systems improved the same families or
simply activated different corners of the benchmark. One total is too weak a
summary.
