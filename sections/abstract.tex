Agent benchmarks are often summarized with a single success rate per model, but
repository-grounded tasks already expose a richer official distinction between
reaching the benchmark's grading stage and actually satisfying the task
criterion. We study that distinction in \gittaskbench using only the
benchmark's official \processmetric/\resultmetric outputs. Our main cohort is a
same-harness open-model sweep over the full official task set. We supplement it
with a shared-task \texttt{Trafilatura\_01/02} slice that extends coverage to
provider models including \texttt{gpt-5.2} and \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}, a
targeted provider screen across
\texttt{gpt-5-chat}, \texttt{gpt-5.2}, and \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}, and one
set of full-benchmark provider sweeps for the same three models. Three empirical patterns emerge. First,
official \resultmetric success is narrow and domain-skewed: in the comparable
cohort, all observed \resultmetric mass lies in office, security, web, and
speech families, while image and video families are complete zeros. Second,
tied headline scores hide family-specific competence: the three headline
leaders in the comparable cohort tie on official \resultmetric success, but
they do so on materially different task families. The provider screen further
shows that broader frontier coverage
does not behave like one uniform control condition, and the full-benchmark
provider sweeps show that short screens can both overstate robustness and miss
where provider gains actually land. Third, official
\processmetric$=$True,
\resultmetric$=$False rows are not uniformly distributed; they cluster in
structured extraction families such as \texttt{Eparse}, \texttt{PDFPlumber},
\texttt{Scrapy}, and \texttt{NeuroKit}. These findings support a narrow
measurement claim: repo-grounded agent evaluation should report the benchmark's
official \processmetric/\resultmetric split and family-level slices, not only a
single global pass rate. This is a descriptive benchmark study rather than a
public-leaderboard or frontier-ranking paper.
