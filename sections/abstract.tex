Agent benchmarks for repository-grounded tasks are typically reduced to a
single global success rate, yet richer structure already exists in their
official outputs. Using \gittaskbench as a case study, we audit its official
\processmetric/\resultmetric split---where \processmetric marks whether a trace
reaches grading and \resultmetric marks whether it satisfies the task
criterion---across a comparable open-model cohort and supplementary
provider-model evaluations. Four patterns emerge. First, official success is
narrow and domain-skewed, concentrating in office-document, security, web, and
speech families while image and video produce none. Second, models that tie on
global totals succeed on different task families, revealing hidden
specialization. Third, provider models do not form one uniform control
condition: they activate different families, and targeted screens can both
overstate and miss activations found in full-benchmark sweeps. Fourth, the
intermediate \processmetric$=$True, \resultmetric$=$False regime clusters in
structured-extraction families rather than appearing as random residue. These
findings recommend that repo-grounded evaluations report the official
\processmetric/\resultmetric split and family-level slices alongside any global
pass rate.
