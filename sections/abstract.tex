Agent benchmarks are often summarized with a single success rate per model, but
repository-grounded tasks already expose a richer official distinction between
reaching the benchmark's grading stage and actually satisfying the task
criterion. We study that distinction in \gittaskbench using only the
benchmark's official \processmetric/\resultmetric outputs. Our main cohort is a
same-harness local open-model sweep over the full official task set. We
supplement it with a shared-task \texttt{Trafilatura\_01/02} slice that extends
coverage to locally available provider models including \texttt{gpt-5.2} and
\texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}, a targeted provider screen across
\texttt{gpt-5-chat}, \texttt{gpt-5.2}, and \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}, and one
full-benchmark \texttt{gpt-5-chat} run. Three empirical patterns emerge. First,
official \resultmetric success is narrow and domain-skewed: in the comparable
local cohort, all observed \resultmetric mass lies in office, security, web,
and speech families, while image and video families are complete zeros. Second,
tied headline scores hide family-specific competence: the leading Qwen models
tie on official \resultmetric success, but they do so on materially different
task families. The provider screen further shows that broader frontier coverage
does not behave like one uniform control condition, and the full-benchmark
\texttt{gpt-5-chat} run shows that the screen overstates how uniformly those
gains extend across task families. Third, official
\processmetric$=$True,
\resultmetric$=$False rows are not uniformly distributed; they cluster in
structured extraction families such as \texttt{Eparse}, \texttt{PDFPlumber},
\texttt{Scrapy}, and \texttt{NeuroKit}. These findings support a narrow
measurement claim: repo-grounded agent evaluation should report the benchmark's
official \processmetric/\resultmetric split and family-level slices, not only a
single global pass rate. This is a development study on local runs rather than
a public-leaderboard or frontier-ranking paper.
