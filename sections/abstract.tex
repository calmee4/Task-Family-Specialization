Agent benchmarks are often summarized with a single success rate per model, but
repository-grounded tasks already expose a richer official distinction between
reaching the benchmark's grading stage and actually satisfying the task
criterion. We study that distinction in \gittaskbench using only the
benchmark's official \processmetric/\resultmetric outputs. Our main cohort is a
comparable local open-model sweep covering $8$ models, $54$ tasks, and $432$
model-task traces. We add a broader shared-task slice on
\texttt{Trafilatura\_01/02} that extends coverage to $10$ locally available
models, including \texttt{gpt-5.2} and \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}, plus a targeted
$14$-task provider screen across \texttt{gpt-5-chat}, \texttt{gpt-5.2}, and
\texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}. Three empirical patterns emerge. First,
official \resultmetric success is narrow and domain-skewed: in the open-8
cohort, all observed \resultmetric mass lies in office, security, web, and
speech families, while image and video families are complete zeros. Second,
tied headline scores hide family-specific competence: three Qwen models all
reach $5/54$ official \resultmetric success, but they do so on materially
different task families. The provider screen further shows that broader frontier
coverage does not behave like one uniform control condition. Third, official
\processmetric$=$True,
\resultmetric$=$False rows are not uniformly distributed; they cluster in
structured extraction families such as \texttt{Eparse}, \texttt{PDFPlumber},
\texttt{Scrapy}, and \texttt{NeuroKit}. These findings support a narrow
measurement claim rather than a new benchmark or a new metric:
repo-grounded agent evaluation should report the benchmark's official
\processmetric/\resultmetric split and family-level slices, not only a single
global pass rate.
