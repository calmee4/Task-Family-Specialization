\section{Results}

\subsection{Global Totals Are Narrow and Domain-Skewed}

Table~\ref{tab:model-summary} gives the exact official totals for the comparable
cohort. Several leading models tie at the top, while three models remain at zero
on both official fields. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} visualizes these totals, and
Table~\ref{tab:domain-summary} breaks them down by domain. The picture is
sharply uneven: office-document and security families contribute most of the
observed \resultmetric success, web and speech contribute a smaller residue,
physiological signals reach grading without \resultmetric success, and image and
video are complete zeros.

The imbalance is not an artifact of domain size. Image tasks constitute the
single largest domain block in the comparable cohort, yet they contribute
nothing to observed official success. Office and security are much smaller by
trace count but account for nearly all \resultmetric success.
Figure~\ref{fig:domain-rates} confirms that this conclusion survives
normalization by domain mass. A single global score therefore blurs together at
least three qualitatively different states: domains with repeatable task
completion, domains with gradeable-but-unsuccessful traces, and domains with no
observed traction at all.

\subsection{Tied Headline Scores Hide Family Specialization}

The three headline leaders in the comparable cohort tie on official
\resultmetric success, but they succeed on different task families.
\texttt{Qwen2.5-14B} reaches official success in \texttt{Eparse},
\texttt{PDFPlumber}, and \texttt{Faker}. \texttt{Qwen2.5-32B} keeps those
families and adds \texttt{SpeechBrain}. \texttt{Qwen3-32B} swaps
\texttt{Eparse} for \texttt{Trafilatura}. Figure~\ref{fig:task-heatmap} makes
this visible at task level. \texttt{Eparse\_02}, \texttt{Faker\_03}, and
\texttt{PDFPlumber\_03} are repeatable successes across multiple models, but
other wins are singleton or near-singleton events such as
\texttt{Trafilatura\_01} for \texttt{Qwen3-32B} and \texttt{Trafilatura\_03}
for \texttt{Mistral-Small-24B}.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figure_task_heatmap.pdf}
    \caption{Official \resultmetric task heatmap for all tasks with at least one observed success in the comparable cohort. Several models tie globally, but they do not win on the same task set.}
    \label{fig:task-heatmap}
\end{figure}

Because absolute success counts are small, we cannot rule out that some
singleton wins reflect stochastic variation rather than stable specialization.
However, two observations suggest the pattern is not purely random. First,
several task-level successes are repeatable across models:
\texttt{Eparse\_02}, \texttt{Faker\_03}, and \texttt{PDFPlumber\_03} are solved
by multiple models independently, indicating that these tasks are reliably
within reach rather than lucky one-offs. Second, the family-level clustering
persists across cohorts: the open-model breadth cohort, the provider screen,
and the full-benchmark sweeps all reproduce the same concentration of success
in office-document and security families, which would not be expected if the
disjoint success sets were driven by random noise alone.

This observation is not limited to Qwen models. \texttt{Mistral-Small-24B}
wins on \texttt{Eparse\_02} and \texttt{Trafilatura\_03} in the comparable
cohort. The shared \texttt{Trafilatura} slice shows that \texttt{gpt-5.2} and
\texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro} both achieve official success on
\texttt{Trafilatura\_02} (Table~\ref{tab:shared-trafilatura}), and the
open-model breadth cohort reproduces the same family concentration with
\texttt{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B} concentrating its six
\resultmetric-positive rows in \texttt{Faker}, \texttt{PDFPlumber}, and
\texttt{Trafilatura}.

\subsection{Provider Models Are Not One Uniform Control Condition}

The comparable cohort and shared \texttt{Trafilatura} slice are dominated by
open-weight models. To test whether the family picture changes under provider
models, we evaluate \texttt{gpt-5-chat}, \texttt{gpt-5.2}, and
\texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro} at two granularities: a targeted task subset and a
full-benchmark sweep.

\paragraph{Targeted subset.}
Table~\ref{tab:provider-screen} shows the results. \texttt{gpt-5-chat} leads
on both official fields and spans the broadest family footprint.
\texttt{gpt-5.2} follows with a slightly narrower footprint.
\texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro} reaches official success only on
\texttt{PDFPlumber\_01}. Both GPT-family models convert \texttt{Scrapy\_03} and
\texttt{NeuroKit\_01} into official \resultmetric success---families that were
zero or graded-but-unsuccessful in the comparable cohort.
\texttt{gpt-5-chat} additionally reaches \texttt{SpeechBrain\_05};
\texttt{gpt-5.2} does not. Provider strength is itself family-selective.

\input{tables/provider_screen}

The subset also reveals two kinds of zeros. \texttt{NeuroKit} and
\texttt{Scrapy} move from zero to \resultmetric-positive under provider models,
suggesting family-specific capability gaps that shift with model class. By
contrast, the image and video stressors (\texttt{AnimeGANv3\_01},
\texttt{VideoPose3D\_01}) remain \processmetric$=$False for all three provider
models, suggesting benchmark-wide dead zones under the current agent setup.
Even with stronger totals, the intermediate \processmetric$=$True,
\resultmetric$=$False regime persists: both GPT-family models still land there
on \texttt{PDFPlumber\_02} and \texttt{Trafilatura\_01}.

\paragraph{Full-benchmark sweeps.}
Table~\ref{tab:provider-full54-summary} shows what changes once the full task
distribution is restored. \texttt{gpt-5.2} records higher official
\processmetric and \resultmetric totals than \texttt{gpt-5-chat} and activates
a much broader family footprint, including \texttt{FunASR},
\texttt{InvisibleWatermark}, \texttt{NeuroKit}, \texttt{SpeechBrain},
\texttt{Stegano}, \texttt{StyleTransfer}, and \texttt{TransparentBackground}.
\texttt{gpt-5-chat} remains concentrated in office, security, web, and one
image family. \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro} stays narrow, centered on \texttt{Faker}
and \texttt{PyPDF2}.

\input{tables/provider_full54_summary}

The targeted subset both overstates and misses activations.
\texttt{gpt-5-chat}'s subset successes on \texttt{SpeechBrain\_05} and
\texttt{NeuroKit\_01} disappear once the full family distribution is restored.
Conversely, \texttt{gpt-5.2} picks up \texttt{FunASR}, \texttt{Stegano},
\texttt{StyleTransfer}, and \texttt{TransparentBackground} only in the full
sweep. A short targeted subset can reveal that a family is reachable at all,
but only full-benchmark sweeps show whether those activations persist, swap, or
disappear.

\subsection{The \processmetric$=$True, \resultmetric$=$False Regime Clusters}

The comparable cohort contains a visible band of official
\processmetric$=$True, \resultmetric$=$False outcomes that are not spread
evenly across the benchmark. They cluster in \texttt{Eparse},
\texttt{PDFPlumber}, \texttt{Scrapy}, and \texttt{NeuroKit}. The benchmark's
official comments show that this regime is heterogeneous: on
\texttt{PDFPlumber\_02}, multiple models reach grading but miss the required
$75\%$ cell-level threshold (e.g., $64.92\%$ for \texttt{Qwen2.5-14B}); on
\texttt{Scrapy\_03}, the official comment reports XML parsing failure; on
\texttt{Eparse\_03}, one comment reports an evaluation-side error after grading
begins.

\input{tables/process_gap_examples}

Table~\ref{tab:process-gap-examples} shows four representative cases. Two
patterns stand out. First, not all graded-but-unsuccessful rows are equal: some
are close threshold misses (\texttt{PDFPlumber\_02}), while others are schema
or parser breakdowns. Second, official comments already separate these cases
without any new judge or taxonomy, making the regime useful for benchmark
reporting even before richer trajectory-level diagnosis.
